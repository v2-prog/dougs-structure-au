Three income scenarios

Income ranges for people aged 45 to 49 who lodged a 2022–23 tax return. Lower is the bottom 10%, middle is the 40th to 60th percentile and higher is the top 10%. Figures are taxable income a year, in 2022–23 dollars, and show where you sit in this age group.

Bottom 10%

Lower income

Up to about $19,500 a year

Less money overall, but costs fall as children leave. Planning for retirement is still possible, with small steady steps.

If this is you

  • Consolidate super accounts and review fees.
  • Check Age Pension rules so you know what to expect at 67.

Professionals to consider: Financial counsellor (free, through the National Debt Helpline), Services Australia Financial Information Service officer, community legal centre.

40th to 60th percentile

Middle income

$63,000 to $91,500 a year

Income is steady while the mortgage shrinks and children become independent. The window for building super is shorter, so it pays to use it.

If this is you

  • Aim to finish the mortgage by the time you plan to stop work.
  • Compare a super top-up with paying the mortgage down faster.

Professionals to consider: Licensed financial adviser (retirement planning), registered tax agent, mortgage broker, your super fund's member adviser.

Top 10%

Higher income

Above about $180,000 a year

Peak earnings in the top tenth. Catch-up super, investments and a plan for how work will wind down all matter.

If this is you

  • Model retirement income with an adviser, including how much super is enough.
  • Review estate planning, including trusts and super death benefits.

Professionals to consider: Licensed financial adviser (CFP), accountant (CA or CPA), SMSF specialist, estate planning lawyer.

Source: ATO Taxation statistics 2022–23, Individuals Table 3 (all individuals who lodged a return, men and women combined). Percentiles are interpolated within the ATO income ranges. Taxable income is after deductions and before tax. People who did not lodge a return are not counted. Examples only, not advice. Check advisers on ASIC's Financial Advisers Register and tax agents on the Tax Practitioners Board register.
