Five economic considerations

Learner licence

Learner permit, logbook hours and driving lessons cost money before a car does.

Sourceaustralia.gov.au

Part-time work

Weekend and after-school shifts pay junior rates that rise with each birthday.

SourceFair Work Ombudsman

Tax and TFN

Earnings under the tax-free threshold usually mean a refund if a return is lodged.

SourceAustralian Taxation Office

First savings goal

Saving for a car, a trip or a laptop builds the savings habit.

SourceMoneySmart (ASIC)

Choosing a pathway

Year 11 and 12 choices lead to university, TAFE, an apprenticeship or work.

SourceJobs and Skills Australia

Three income scenarios

Illustrative gross incomes: lower about $50,000, middle about $100,000, higher about $200,000 a year. For ages under 18, read these as household incomes. For retirement ages, read them as lower, middle and higher income and savings. They are examples, not official brackets.

~ $50,000 a year

Lower income

A part-time job is likely needed to pay for the car, the phone and some school costs. Lessons may be shared with a supervising family driver.

~ $100,000 a year

Middle income

Work pays for fuel and outings, while the family helps with lessons and insurance. A savings goal is set for a first car.

~ $200,000 a year

Higher income

A car may be gifted or heavily subsidised. Study pathways can include private tuition, exchange years or interstate boarding.
