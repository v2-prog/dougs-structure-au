Three income scenarios

Income ranges for people aged 18 to 24 who lodged a 2022–23 tax return. Lower is the bottom 10%, middle is the 40th to 60th percentile and higher is the top 10%. Figures are taxable income a year, in 2022–23 dollars, and show where you sit in this age group.

Bottom 10%

Lower income

Up to about $8,900 a year

Share-house rent, a used car and study costs can use all of a low income. Credit cards and buy now pay later are a risk when spare cash is thin.

If this is you

  • Check Rent Assistance and Youth Allowance if you qualify.
  • Call a free financial counsellor early if bills or repayments start to slip.

Professionals to consider: Financial counsellor (free, through the National Debt Helpline, 1800 007 007), ATO Tax Help volunteer, Services Australia Financial Information Service officer.

40th to 60th percentile

Middle income

$28,000 to $40,500 a year

Steady work leaves room to save alongside rent, car and travel. Study debt can start showing on payslips once income passes the HELP threshold.

If this is you

  • Build an emergency fund of at least a month of costs.
  • Compare car loans and insurance before buying; the lowest repayment is not always the lowest cost.

Professionals to consider: Registered tax agent, your super fund's member adviser, a mortgage broker when you start to buy.

Top 10%

Higher income

Above about $71,500 a year

Top-tenth earnings at this age often come from trades, mining, sales or a business. Tax on higher income arrives faster than expected.

If this is you

  • Set aside tax if income is not taxed at source.
  • Consider salary sacrificing a modest amount into super.

Professionals to consider: Licensed financial adviser, accountant, mortgage broker for larger or more complex loans.

Source: ATO Taxation statistics 2022–23, Individuals Table 3 (all individuals who lodged a return, men and women combined). Percentiles are interpolated within the ATO income ranges. Taxable income is after deductions and before tax. People who did not lodge a return are not counted. Examples only, not advice. Check advisers on ASIC's Financial Advisers Register and tax agents on the Tax Practitioners Board register.
