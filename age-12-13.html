Five economic considerations

First bike

A first bike or scooter is usually a family purchase, with a helmet and repairs on top.

SourceMoneySmart (ASIC)

Pocket money

Chores and gifts create the first income and the first choice between saving and spending.

SourceMoneySmart (ASIC)

Clubs and hobbies

Sport fees, uniforms and gear are often the first regular cost per child.

Sourceaustralia.gov.au

Online life

Games and apps bring in-app purchases and the first exposure to online scams.

SourceScamwatch (ACCC)

Starting high school

Uniform, laptop, stationery and excursions often land in the same year.

SourceDepartment of Education

Three income scenarios

Income ranges for young people under 18 who lodged a 2022–23 tax return, mostly from part-time work. Lower is the bottom 10%, middle is the 40th to 60th percentile and higher is the top 10%. Figures are taxable income a year, in 2022–23 dollars, and show where you sit in this age group. Household income is not in this table, so these are the young person's own earnings; family income decides most of what is possible.

Bottom 10%

Lower income

Up to about $1,500 a year

Little or no personal income at this age. Pocket money, gifts and small jobs for family are the main sources, so the household budget decides most of what is possible.

If this is you

  • Ask the school about subsidised activities and help with uniforms, camps and devices.
  • A fee-free youth savings account lets small amounts build up without charges.

Professionals to consider: School careers adviser, youth worker, Fair Work Ombudsman for pay questions.

40th to 60th percentile

Middle income

$6,200 to $10,000 a year

Pocket money and the odd paid job, such as lawn mowing or pet minding, build the first savings habit. Most spending decisions still sit with the family.

If this is you

  • Split any money received into spend, save and give to start the habit.
  • Talk through online purchases and in-app spending before they happen.

Professionals to consider: School careers adviser, bank youth-account staff, a registered tax agent for a first return.

Top 10%

Higher income

Above about $23,500 a year

Earnings from small jobs or online activity can already exceed pocket money. Income earned in a child's name can be taxed differently from an adult's, so keep records from the start.

If this is you

  • Ask a tax agent how income in a child's name is taxed before opening investments for them.
  • Set clear family rules for spending and saving, even when money is plentiful.

Professionals to consider: Registered tax agent or accountant (income in a child's name, a small business), licensed financial adviser (family wealth and education planning).

Source: ATO Taxation statistics 2022–23, Individuals Table 3 (all individuals who lodged a return, men and women combined). Percentiles are interpolated within the ATO income ranges. Taxable income is after deductions and before tax. People who did not lodge a return are not counted. Examples only, not advice. Check advisers on ASIC's Financial Advisers Register and tax agents on the Tax Practitioners Board register.
