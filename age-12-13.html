Five economic considerations

First bike

A first bike or scooter is usually a family purchase, with a helmet and repairs on top.

SourceMoneySmart (ASIC)

Pocket money

Chores and gifts create the first income and the first choice between saving and spending.

SourceMoneySmart (ASIC)

Clubs and hobbies

Sport fees, uniforms and gear are often the first regular cost per child.

Sourceaustralia.gov.au

Online life

Games and apps bring in-app purchases and the first exposure to online scams.

SourceScamwatch (ACCC)

Starting high school

Uniform, laptop, stationery and excursions often land in the same year.

SourceDepartment of Education

Three income scenarios

Illustrative gross incomes: lower about $50,000, middle about $100,000, higher about $200,000 a year. For ages under 18, read these as household incomes. For retirement ages, read them as lower, middle and higher income and savings. They are examples, not official brackets.

~ $50,000 a year

Lower income

Household budget is tight, so one club, a second-hand bike and hand-me-down uniforms are common. School and sport vouchers can cover part of the cost.

~ $100,000 a year

Middle income

Room for one or two activities, a new bike and a phone plan paid by the family. Pocket money mostly goes on small treats.

~ $200,000 a year

Higher income

Several activities, coaching, and a school with higher fees. Savings accounts or investments in a child's name may be opened early.
