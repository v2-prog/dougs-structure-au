Three income scenarios

Income ranges for people aged 50 to 54 who lodged a 2022–23 tax return. Lower is the bottom 10%, middle is the 40th to 60th percentile and higher is the top 10%. Figures are taxable income a year, in 2022–23 dollars, and show where you sit in this age group.

Bottom 10%

Lower income

Up to about $19,000 a year

Focus on clearing debts and any available catch-up contributions. Even small amounts help over the next 15 years.

If this is you

  • Ask the lender about hardship arrangements if repayments are a strain.
  • Check you can access any super insurance cover if work is affected by health.

Professionals to consider: Financial counsellor (free, through the National Debt Helpline), Services Australia Financial Information Service officer, community legal centre.

40th to 60th percentile

Middle income

$61,000 to $88,000 a year

Peak working years. Mortgage payoff, super catch-up and support for adult children often compete.

If this is you

  • Set a mortgage-free target date and a super target.
  • Be careful guaranteeing children's loans without advice.

Professionals to consider: Licensed financial adviser (retirement planning), registered tax agent, mortgage broker, your super fund's member adviser.

Top 10%

Higher income

Above about $178,000 a year

High income gives room for catch-up contributions and investments. Timing of work reduction and super drawdown need planning.

If this is you

  • Consider spouse contribution splitting where it helps.
  • Get specialist tax and super advice before moving large sums.

Professionals to consider: Licensed financial adviser (CFP), accountant (CA or CPA), SMSF specialist, estate planning lawyer.

Source: ATO Taxation statistics 2022–23, Individuals Table 3 (all individuals who lodged a return, men and women combined). Percentiles are interpolated within the ATO income ranges. Taxable income is after deductions and before tax. People who did not lodge a return are not counted. Examples only, not advice. Check advisers on ASIC's Financial Advisers Register and tax agents on the Tax Practitioners Board register.
