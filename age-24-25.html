24–25

Bronze row · ages 22 to 31

Five economic considerations

Step up

First job switches usually give the largest pay jumps of a career.

SourceJobs and Skills Australia

Emergency fund

Three months of expenses is a common target, held somewhere easy to access.

SourceMoneySmart (ASIC)

Insurance basics

Health cover, income protection and car cover. Medicare levy surcharge and loading rules start to matter.

SourceDepartment of Health

Debt habits

Credit cards and buy now pay later can erode savings if not cleared each month.

SourceMoneySmart (ASIC)

Relationships

Partners often begin to share rent and costs.

Three income scenarios

Illustrative gross incomes: lower about $50,000, middle about $100,000, higher about $200,000 a year. For ages under 18, read these as household incomes. For retirement ages, read them as lower, middle and higher income and savings. They are examples, not official brackets.

~ $50,000 a year

Lower income

An emergency fund of even one month is hard to build. Clearing high-interest debt first usually pays best.

~ $100,000 a year

Middle income

Three months in savings, a share of rent covered by a partner, and extra super contributions are within reach.

~ $200,000 a year

Higher income

Most pay goes toward saving or investing. Private health cover is chosen deliberately to avoid surcharge and loading.
