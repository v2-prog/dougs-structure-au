14–15

Mountain View row · ages 12 to 21

Five economic considerations

First casual job

Many states allow work from 14 or 15 in set roles, paid at junior rates.

SourceFair Work Ombudsman

First bank account

A youth account, a debit card and a Tax File Number so tax is not withheld at the top rate.

SourceAustralian Taxation Office

Own phone and plan

Handset, plan and accessories are usually still paid by parents.

SourceMoneySmart (ASIC)

Spending money

Clothes, food and social outings start to come from own earnings.

SourceMoneySmart (ASIC)

Subject choices

Choices about subjects and vocational courses start to point toward later pay.

SourceJobs and Skills Australia

  • Tax File Number application (ATO)
  • Young workers guide (Fair Work)
  • State work-permit rules for under-16s

Three income scenarios

Illustrative gross incomes: lower about $50,000, middle about $100,000, higher about $200,000 a year. For ages under 18, read these as household incomes. For retirement ages, read them as lower, middle and higher income and savings. They are examples, not official brackets.

~ $50,000 a year

Lower income

Casual work may help cover school costs and the phone bill. Family income can affect eligibility for school-related support.

~ $100,000 a year

Middle income

Earnings are mostly the teenager's own spending money. A bank account with a parent is used to build a first savings habit.

~ $200,000 a year

Higher income

Work may be about experience rather than need. Tutoring, camps and an upgraded phone are usually funded by the family.
