Five economic considerations

First casual job

Many states allow work from 14 or 15 in set roles, paid at junior rates.

SourceFair Work Ombudsman

First bank account

A youth account, a debit card and a Tax File Number so tax is not withheld at the top rate.

SourceAustralian Taxation Office

Own phone and plan

Handset, plan and accessories are usually still paid by parents.

SourceMoneySmart (ASIC)

Spending money

Clothes, food and social outings start to come from own earnings.

SourceMoneySmart (ASIC)

Subject choices

Choices about subjects and vocational courses start to point toward later pay.

SourceJobs and Skills Australia

Three income scenarios

Income ranges for young people under 18 who lodged a 2022–23 tax return, mostly from part-time work. Lower is the bottom 10%, middle is the 40th to 60th percentile and higher is the top 10%. Figures are taxable income a year, in 2022–23 dollars, and show where you sit in this age group. Household income is not in this table, so these are the young person's own earnings; family income decides most of what is possible.

Bottom 10%

Lower income

Up to about $1,500 a year

First casual work such as babysitting, deliveries or hospitality is often the whole income. Pay is small, so the Tax File Number and the phone plan are the first real money decisions.

If this is you

  • Apply for a Tax File Number so an employer does not withhold tax at the highest rate.
  • Check youth pay rates with the Fair Work Ombudsman if pay seems low.

Professionals to consider: School careers adviser, youth worker, Fair Work Ombudsman for pay questions.

40th to 60th percentile

Middle income

$6,200 to $10,000 a year

Regular weekend or after-school shifts can cover the phone, clothes and outings. Income at this level usually attracts little or no tax because of the tax-free threshold.

If this is you

  • Claim the tax-free threshold on one job only when filling in the TFN declaration.
  • Open a separate savings account so wages do not mix with spending.

Professionals to consider: School careers adviser, bank youth-account staff, a registered tax agent for a first return.

Top 10%

Higher income

Above about $23,500 a year

Income here is well above most teenagers and can approach the tax-free threshold. Super may be paid on top of wages if the young person works more than 30 hours a week.

If this is you

  • Keep payslips and records for a first tax return.
  • Ask a tax agent about the first return and about any investments held for the young person.

Professionals to consider: Registered tax agent or accountant (income in a child's name, a small business), licensed financial adviser (family wealth and education planning).

Source: ATO Taxation statistics 2022–23, Individuals Table 3 (all individuals who lodged a return, men and women combined). Percentiles are interpolated within the ATO income ranges. Taxable income is after deductions and before tax. People who did not lodge a return are not counted. Examples only, not advice. Check advisers on ASIC's Financial Advisers Register and tax agents on the Tax Practitioners Board register.
