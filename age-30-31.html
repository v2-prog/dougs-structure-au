Three income scenarios

Income ranges for people aged 30 to 34 who lodged a 2022–23 tax return. Lower is the bottom 10%, middle is the 40th to 60th percentile and higher is the top 10%. Figures are taxable income a year, in 2022–23 dollars, and show where you sit in this age group.

Bottom 10%

Lower income

Up to about $16,000 a year

Buying may only be possible with a partner, a scheme or a lower-priced region. Childcare costs and variable work can strain the budget.

If this is you

  • Check Family Tax Benefit and Child Care Subsidy if you have children.
  • Ask lenders about hardship options early if repayments feel tight.

Professionals to consider: Financial counsellor (free, through the National Debt Helpline), Services Australia Financial Information Service officer, community legal centre.

40th to 60th percentile

Middle income

$53,500 to $75,500 a year

A first home with a sizeable mortgage is typical, so repayments take a large share of income and a buffer is important.

If this is you

  • Review the mortgage rate each year with a broker or by asking the lender.
  • Write a will and nominate super beneficiaries once a partner or child is involved.

Professionals to consider: Mortgage broker, registered tax agent, licensed financial adviser (fee for service), insurance broker, estate planning lawyer for a will.

Top 10%

Higher income

Above about $134,000 a year

A larger deposit means lower repayments. Offset accounts, extra repayments and starting investments are options, and tax planning starts to matter.

If this is you

  • Consider salary sacrificing into super to lower taxable income.
  • Look at life and income protection cover beyond what super provides.

Professionals to consider: Licensed financial adviser (CFP), accountant (CA or CPA), tax specialist, estate planning lawyer.

Source: ATO Taxation statistics 2022–23, Individuals Table 3 (all individuals who lodged a return, men and women combined). Percentiles are interpolated within the ATO income ranges. Taxable income is after deductions and before tax. People who did not lodge a return are not counted. Examples only, not advice. Check advisers on ASIC's Financial Advisers Register and tax agents on the Tax Practitioners Board register.
