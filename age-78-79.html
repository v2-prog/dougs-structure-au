Three income scenarios

Income ranges for people aged 75 and over who lodged a 2022–23 tax return. Lower is the bottom 10%, middle is the 40th to 60th percentile and higher is the top 10%. Figures are taxable income a year, in 2022–23 dollars, and show where you sit in this age group. Tax-free super payments from age 60 are not counted in taxable income, so real spending power can be higher than the figures shown.

Bottom 10%

Lower income

Up to about $3,000 a year

Subsidised care covers most costs, but waiting times can be long. Early assessment helps.

If this is you

  • Ask My Aged Care about home support and care options.
  • Use free advocacy services if you need help with care decisions.

Professionals to consider: My Aged Care assessor, Older Persons Advocacy Network (1800 700 600), Services Australia Financial Information Service officer, a seniors legal or rights service in your state.

40th to 60th percentile

Middle income

$21,000 to $33,000 a year

Care at home or in a facility is the biggest cost to plan for. Fees depend on income and assets.

If this is you

  • Compare aged care providers and ask for written fee details.
  • Talk to a specialist aged care adviser before choosing how to pay accommodation costs.

Professionals to consider: Specialist aged care financial adviser, elder law solicitor, My Aged Care assessor, registered tax agent.

Top 10%

Higher income

Above about $98,000 a year

Higher income and assets mean higher means-tested fees. Choices about accommodation payments, the home and the estate are the main issues.

If this is you

  • Compare a lump sum accommodation deposit with periodic payments.
  • Make sure wills and powers of attorney are current.

Professionals to consider: Specialist aged care financial adviser, elder law and estate planning lawyer, accountant, licensed financial adviser.

Source: ATO Taxation statistics 2022–23, Individuals Table 3 (all individuals who lodged a return, men and women combined). Percentiles are interpolated within the ATO income ranges. Taxable income is after deductions and before tax. People who did not lodge a return are not counted. Examples only, not advice. Check advisers on ASIC's Financial Advisers Register and tax agents on the Tax Practitioners Board register.
