Five economic considerations

Childcare costs

Childcare costs peak in these years. The Child Care Subsidy offsets part, depending on income.

Return to work

Often part-time at first, which affects super and pay progression.

Three income scenarios

Illustrative gross incomes: lower about $50,000, middle about $100,000, higher about $200,000 a year. For ages under 18, read these as household incomes. For retirement ages, read them as lower, middle and higher income and savings. They are examples, not official brackets.

~ $50,000 a year

Lower income

Childcare can cost nearly as much as one wage, so the subsidy and work hours need careful planning.

~ $100,000 a year

Middle income

Two incomes cover childcare and a mortgage, but there is little left over. Taking a break from work is a real trade-off.

~ $200,000 a year

Higher income

Childcare, nanny or private options are affordable. Extra mortgage repayments and investing continue.
