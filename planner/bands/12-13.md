# Ages 12–13: Foundations & Financial Literacy

**Educational only. Not personal advice. Verify current rules on official Australian sites.**

## Life Events & Cumulative Milestones
- Starting high school (Year 7–8 in most states).
- First pocket money, chores, or part-time work (babysitting, paper round).
- Learning budgeting, saving, and the difference between needs and wants.
- Parents may open a youth bank account or discuss family finances.
- Superannuation concept introduced (employer contributions begin later).

## Scenarios
### Scenario A: School + family support
Focus on literacy through school programs or Moneysmart resources. Parents model saving.

### Scenario B: Early work
Small income from casual work. Opportunity to open TFN and start basic tracking.

### Scenario C: Eco / community interest
Early exposure to cooperative ideas, gardening, or community projects (Structure Lab alignment).

## Alternative Living Arrangements
- Living with parents (standard).
- Multi-generational household.
- Regional or rural lifestyle with lower costs.

## Wealth Level Snapshot (end of band)
| Level | Savings / Assets | Notes |
|-------|------------------|-------|
| Modest | $0–200 | Pocket money only |
| Comfortable track | $200–1,000 | Consistent saving habits |
| Ahead | > $1,000 | Family support or early work |

## Structure Lab Elements
Early introduction to household roles and continuity concepts.

## Key Actions & Checklist
- Open a youth savings account.
- Track simple income and expenses.
- Discuss compound interest with family.
- Explore free resources: Moneysmart.gov.au.

Official sources: Moneysmart, ATO (TFN), school financial literacy programs.
