Three income scenarios

Income ranges for people aged 35 to 39 who lodged a 2022–23 tax return. Lower is the bottom 10%, middle is the 40th to 60th percentile and higher is the top 10%. Figures are taxable income a year, in 2022–23 dollars, and show where you sit in this age group.

Bottom 10%

Lower income

Up to about $17,000 a year

School costs are the main squeeze. School-card schemes, subsidised activities and concession cards can ease them.

If this is you

  • Ask the school about financial assistance for fees, excursions and devices.
  • Set a simple budget that covers the fixed costs first.

Professionals to consider: Financial counsellor (free, through the National Debt Helpline), Services Australia Financial Information Service officer, community legal centre.

40th to 60th percentile

Middle income

$58,000 to $83,500 a year

School fees, activities and mortgage payments take much of the budget. Saving for each need separately is more realistic than one big pot.

If this is you

  • Compare the costs of school choices before committing.
  • Add to super when a pay rise arrives, not only when the budget allows.

Professionals to consider: Mortgage broker, registered tax agent, licensed financial adviser (fee for service), insurance broker, estate planning lawyer for a will.

Top 10%

Higher income

Above about $157,000 a year

Private school fees, travel and investment goals are manageable. Estate planning and guardianship in a will now deserve attention.

If this is you

  • Review wills and powers of attorney with a lawyer.
  • Check both partners are making good use of their super caps and tax rates.

Professionals to consider: Licensed financial adviser (CFP), accountant (CA or CPA), tax specialist, estate planning lawyer.

Source: ATO Taxation statistics 2022–23, Individuals Table 3 (all individuals who lodged a return, men and women combined). Percentiles are interpolated within the ATO income ranges. Taxable income is after deductions and before tax. People who did not lodge a return are not counted. Examples only, not advice. Check advisers on ASIC's Financial Advisers Register and tax agents on the Tax Practitioners Board register.
